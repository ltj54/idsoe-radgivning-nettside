POTENTIAL AND ACHIEVEMENT

Learning potential and the learning environment

It is useful to distinguish between what a child achieves now and what they may be capable of learning and developing. High grades can make some students’ abilities visible. Other students’ potential is less apparent in assignments, assessments or classroom participation.

Learning potential develops in interaction with the environment. An interest in a subject needs encouragement, appropriate challenges and opportunities to practise. Finding learning easy does not mean that further development happens automatically.

Achievement can also change throughout a student’s school years. Previous success does not tell us everything about what a student needs today. An important question is how teaching responds to their current knowledge, interests and needs.

Strengths in one or more subjects

A child may have particular strengths in mathematics, language or science without being equally advanced in every subject. Look at how the student reasons, asks questions and approaches tasks in each subject. Their overall grades may not capture these differences.

A question for reflection

What can students demonstrate through the tasks we set – and what opportunities do they have to show other aspects of what they know?
