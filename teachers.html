For teachers and school communities

Pedagogical differentiation, progression and participation in the community.

High learning potential is not always visible in school achievement. Some students cannot demonstrate what they know in everyday teaching. Examine what the student already knows and what can support further learning.

Explore prior knowledge and learning needs

The Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training describes identification as more than tests and assessments. Consider the student’s experiences, knowledge, skills and development. Include information from parents and others who know the student. High learning potential can also coexist with learning difficulties.

Adapt the challenges, not just the workload

Exploration, project work, depth and feedback can guide further learning. When a student has mastered the content, tasks should provide new understanding. More tasks of the same type do not necessarily represent academic progress.

One teaching example might involve students working on a shared topic while encountering different questions and challenges. One student might explore basic relationships, while another compares explanations, tests a hypothesis or explains which solutions are most appropriate.

Monitor both learning and the experience of inclusion

Assessment should inform the next step. Ask whether the challenge is meaningful and whether support is sufficient. Ask whether there is room for the student’s own ideas. Academic differentiation must also be considered alongside participation and classroom relationships.

  • What has the student learned that they did not know before?
  • Can the student use and develop their own strategies?
  • Are there opportunities for academic collaboration and shared interests?
  • Does the student feel that teaching and the community are working better for them?

This is also work for school leaders and staff: discussing practice, sharing experiences and following up adaptations over time. The Directorate emphasises variation in activities, resources and assessment approaches, and the need for schools to monitor students’ development.
