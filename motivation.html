MOTIVATION AND DIFFERING NEEDS

Motivation and a lack of academic challenge

Boredom, frustration and underachievement can arise when students do not experience appropriate challenges over time. If much of the teaching covers what a student already knows, there may be little room to experience effort, new learning and development.

This gives us reason to examine the learning situation before interpreting limited participation as a lack of interest or willingness. At the same time, children differ. A student’s reaction alone cannot tell us their learning potential or explain why they are struggling.

Children may experience learning, impressions and relationships in different ways. An important starting point is to approach each child with curiosity: what engages them, what feels demanding and what makes learning meaningful? No single personality type describes all students with high learning potential.

When potential is not reflected in achievement

A student may have strong abilities yet show little engagement or achieve low grades. Repetition without new learning and limited experience of demanding tasks are relevant factors to explore. Absence and dropout can have several causes; consider the student’s learning, relationships and wider circumstances together.

Learning how to learn

Students who have long completed tasks with little effort also need opportunities to develop study habits and learning strategies. When work becomes more demanding, they may need support with planning, trying different approaches and accepting that understanding takes time.

An appropriate challenge allows room for mistakes and further effort. The teacher can help the student break down a task, review a strategy and ask for help. Discuss how the student learns, including when the answer does not come immediately.

Look for connections

Does engagement change when the student can pursue an interest in depth, explore a new question or work on a more challenging task?
