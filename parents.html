For parents

Understanding your child’s strengths, needs and wellbeing across everyday life.

DOES THIS FEEL FAMILIAR?

You want to understand and support your child better

  • Your child asks unusually mature questions, and you wonder whether they have high learning potential.
  • You feel that your child is not understood in kindergarten or at school.
  • You are struggling to find education that provides enough intellectual challenge and stimulation.
  • You are concerned because your child feels different from their peers.
  • Your child has high learning potential as well as a learning difficulty or diagnosis, and you are unsure how to navigate this complexity.
  • You want to support a child who shows perfectionism, unease or anxiety, but are unsure how.
  • You see boredom in kindergarten or school leading to frustration or behavioural difficulties.
  • You are unsure how to communicate your child’s need for appropriate support to the kindergarten or school.

These examples are intended for recognition and reflection. They are not a test or diagnosis.

Does this feel familiar? Please get in touch

As a parent, you may see a child who immerses themselves in their interests, asks many questions or learns quickly in certain areas. The school may describe a student who shows little engagement or cannot demonstrate what they know. These differing accounts are a starting point for exploring the situation together, rather than settling on one explanation in advance.

Consider interests, learning and well-being together

Academic development needs to be considered alongside social and emotional needs. Parents can support curiosity while also allowing space for friendship, play and leisure. The aim is to understand what the child needs, rather than turning their potential into a demand for high achievement.

Talk with the child about what they enjoy learning, what feels too easy or difficult, and when they feel part of a community. Some children miss academic challenges; others particularly want to find people who share their interests. Needs can change over time.

Support an early interest in reading

When a child shows an interest in letters or can already read, adults can provide books and conversations suited to their understanding and interests. Reading can sit alongside play and time with others. The child does not need to be held back to follow the same pace as their peers.

When the child starts school, share what they enjoy reading, what they understand and the questions that texts prompt. This gives the teacher a starting point for further learning.

Make collaboration with the school specific

A practical starting point for a conversation with the school is to gather examples from everyday life and let the child contribute. Describe situations rather than drawing conclusions about particular traits or causes.

  • Which activities engage the child, and what do they learn through them?
  • What does the child say about tasks, ways of working and relationships at school?
  • What does the teacher observe, and where do the experiences match or differ?
  • What adaptation could be tried, and when will you review together how it is working?

These questions are suggestions for dialogue, not a checklist for identifying high learning potential. The aim is a shared understanding of learning needs and well-being.

These topics can support dialogue between home, school and student.

SERVICES FOR PARENTS

Individual guidance on your child’s strengths and needs

We offer individual guidance to parents who want a better understanding of their child’s strengths, needs and opportunities for development.

Using information from the parents and a developmental interview, we prepare a profile of the child’s strengths and needs. Based on this, you may receive:

  • a guidance report with practical advice on supporting your child’s development at home
  • a report recommending relevant adjustments in kindergarten or school
  • a practical support plan for a period of four to six weeks, which the kindergarten or school can try
  • courses and webinars for parent groups about children with high learning potential

Guidance and consultations are available at weekends.

Important

The service provides guidance and support. It does not replace a diagnostic or psychological assessment. Our considerations are based on information from the parents and should, when needed, be supplemented by observations from the kindergarten or school.
