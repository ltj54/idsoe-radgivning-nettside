ADULTS’ KNOWLEDGE

Professional knowledge and collaboration in schools

Adults’ knowledge and an open, inclusive attitude are important. Students’ questions and alternative ideas can be a starting point for further learning. This requires adults to try to understand what the student is expressing.

School leadership and collaboration among colleagues are discussed as important settings for reviewing practice: how do we find out what students know? What do we do when a student has already mastered the content? How do we monitor whether an adaptation is working?

Knowledge from early childhood education onwards and greater attention to the topic in professional education can strengthen this work. Differentiation belongs within a wider aim of supporting different students and enabling participation in the community.

School leadership and organisation

Adaptations need a shared plan. School leaders can allocate time for collaboration, clarify responsibilities and enable coordination across subjects and year groups. At transitions, information about the student’s learning and previous adaptations should be passed on.

Share teaching resources and develop expertise together

A shared collection of open tasks, advanced material and relevant digital tools can support teachers. Staff can discuss which students the activities suit, what guidance they need and what the experiences show.

Udir’s professional development package on high learning potential is a resource for schools and the educational and psychological counselling service (PPT), available in Norwegian.

Resources and follow-up across schools

School authorities and national government influence opportunities through priorities, professional development and access to resources. A useful goal for school development is to monitor both academic progress and students’ experience of participation and belonging.

A shared understanding of learning needs

A shared vocabulary for learning needs can make it easier to examine experiences, discuss support and listen to both students’ and adults’ perspectives.
