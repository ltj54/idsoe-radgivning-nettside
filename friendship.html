FRIENDSHIP AND COMMUNITY

Friendship and shared interests

Meeting others who share interests, questions or a sense of humour can be valuable. The class community matters, but being the same age does not automatically create shared interests.

Projects across year groups and meeting places centred on academic interests are discussed as opportunities for connection and belonging. The key is to understand where the child experiences positive relationships and can participate actively.

Take the student’s experience of bullying seriously

Ask how the student is doing, where they feel unsafe and what support they need. Good grades alone do not tell us how a child is doing socially. Follow-up should reflect the individual student’s situation.

Depth and shared interests

In-depth study, contact with professional communities and a community of other interested children can support both academic and social development. Such opportunities should be considered alongside the school’s work and the child’s own interests.

Development without pressure to perform

Supporting an interest can be about enjoyment, depth and community. A child’s worth does not depend on their achievements.
