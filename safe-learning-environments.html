SAFETY AND RELATIONSHIPS

A safe and predictable learning environment

Children need both academic challenges and adults who take safety and well-being seriously. This applies to students with different learning needs. High learning potential does not in itself mean that a child has experienced trauma.

Make everyday routines understandable

Clear routines can help students know what will happen and what is expected. Explain the activity, prepare for transitions and give notice of changes. Preventing bullying and exclusion belongs alongside this work: students need to feel safe both in lessons and within the community.

Build trust through everyday interactions

Relationships develop when adults are present, show interest and follow through on what they say. A calm greeting, time to listen and an agreed check-in can be practical ways to show care. Ask what the student needs to make the school day work better.

Understand reactions before choosing support

Distressing and traumatic experiences can affect learning, behaviour and relationships. A trauma-sensitive approach means being aware of this possibility while exploring different explanations. Restlessness, withdrawal or difficulty concentrating do not on their own indicate trauma.

Notice when situations become difficult, what is happening around the student and what helps. Combine clear, respectful boundaries with support. Adaptations should reflect the student’s needs and experiences.

Offer choices and ways to express oneself

Conversation, drawing, writing or practical activities can offer different ways to participate and express oneself. Give the student appropriate choices without requiring them to disclose distressing experiences. Everyday classroom activities should not be used as a method for processing traumatic memories.

Work together on further support

Schools can provide safety, adjustments and ongoing support in everyday life. When difficulties affect a student’s functioning, collaboration with relevant professionals may be needed. Assessing and treating trauma reactions requires qualified healthcare professionals.
