PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Ella’s ideas for sharing knowledge and developing practice

How can knowledge about learning potential inform better practice in early childhood education and schools?

Ella has many ideas about developing knowledge with students and professional communities, and about trying out and improving teaching. The ideas concern early knowledge, professional development, student participation and collaboration between research and practice.

Knowledge from early childhood onwards

Ella highlights the importance of adults learning about high learning potential early in a child’s life. The idea is to help early childhood settings understand differing needs and provide appropriate opportunities for learning and exploration.

More knowledge in professional education

She points to higher education as a setting for giving future professionals more knowledge about these children. Teaching and courses on learning potential and differing needs can provide a foundation for professional practice.

Students as co-developers

One idea is for students themselves to help develop activities or courses for professionals. Their experiences of learning, challenges and belonging can help ensure that adults’ professional development is informed by what students experience and need.

Small teaching pilot projects

Ella describes an interest in trying out differentiation in different subjects and examining the experiences systematically. These trials could help identify teaching approaches that work in the Norwegian context.

A research and resource centre

She also outlines the idea of a centre combining research with support for schools and parents. The proposal connects the development of new knowledge with the needs of those who work with and care for children in everyday life.

This presents professional ideas, rather than projects already under way or services available to book. Which ideas Ella would like to develop further through Idsøe Rådgivning remains to be discussed with her.
