FOR KINDERGARTENS

See curiosity, strengths and needs

Kindergarten can be an important place for noticing how a child explores, learns, plays and finds belonging.

High learning potential can appear in many ways. It is not a particular personality type, nor is it a diagnosis.

Observe across situations

Notice the questions a child asks, the topics they return to, how they solve problems and when they show delight or frustration. Look beyond comparisons with age and consider what the child already understands and what creates new learning.

Make room for depth and play

Support may include open questions, varied materials, time for deeper exploration and contact with others who share an interest. The goal is not more work, but meaningful opportunities to explore.

Take safety and bullying seriously

A good provision combines intellectual stimulation with social safety. Adults need to listen to the child’s own experience, especially when they withdraw, hide their interests or face exclusion.

Work with parents

Parents see the child in other situations. Share specific observations and ask what they recognise. Discuss strengths, wellbeing and needs without turning observations into a test or diagnosis.
