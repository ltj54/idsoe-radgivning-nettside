UNDERSTANDING

Learning potential and school achievement

High learning potential is not always reflected in grades or school achievement.

Children may need greater challenges even when their potential is not clearly expressed in everyday school life. Children and students with high learning potential are different from one another. It is therefore important to understand each individual’s interests, experiences and learning needs.

QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

Questions about learning potential

Some central distinctions from the in-depth discussion are brought together here.

Is high learning potential the same as good grades?

No. Grades describe demonstrated achievement in a particular context. It is also important to understand the student’s knowledge, interests, learning environment and opportunities for further learning.

Do students who learn easily also need support?

Learning easily does not mean that a student automatically encounters appropriate challenges. Support may involve deeper study, feedback, academic discussion and opportunities to develop interests with others.

Are more tasks always a useful adaptation?

More tasks do not necessarily lead to new learning if the student has already mastered the content. The starting point should be what they need to learn next and whether tasks allow exploration, greater depth or more complex questions.

How do we know whether a student feels included?

We need to listen to the student’s own experience. Can they contribute academically, find a community and feel understood? Physical presence in the classroom does not give the whole answer.

Does differentiation mean that some children are worth more than others?

No. The issue is differing learning needs, not differences in human worth. Differentiation is about supporting all students and giving them opportunities to develop.
