RECOGNISING LEARNING NEEDS

Explore what the student knows and how they learn

Assessment should guide teaching. A test can show some of what a student has mastered, while observation, conversations and work over time provide a broader picture.

Let students share interests and ideas

Ask what the student enjoys exploring, which questions occupy them and what they would like to make or investigate. Let them share a piece of work or propose a project. This may reveal interests that everyday tasks do not bring out.

Include parents’ observations

Ask for concrete examples of what the child reads, builds, discusses or learns at home. Consider these alongside classroom observations. Differing accounts offer a starting point for further exploration.

Provide several opportunities to show strengths

Use varied tasks and notice the student’s explanations, questions and approaches. Language, previous learning opportunities and co-occurring difficulties can affect what they demonstrate. Give students several opportunities to show what they know.

What can different assessment methods tell us?

Above-level tasks can provide more information when a regular test is too easy. A high score on an easy test reveals little about the extent of a student’s understanding.

Dynamic assessment also explores how a student learns with support, for example through assessment, guided learning and reassessment. This differs from simply using a harder test.

Standardised tests require appropriate expertise and must be used according to their guidelines. Assessment is not a diagnosis or a guarantee of future development.

Read about identification at NAGC and dynamic assessment at ASHA (ASHA’s example concerns language assessment).

From observation to teaching

Choose an appropriate challenge, agree on support and review both learning and well-being. Use the experience to adjust the approach.
